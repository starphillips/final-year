The following is an email sent to Meridian City Council by Meridian Estates Residents' Voice, a community group representing residents of three housing estates affected by SafeStreet, an AI-enabled public safety platform combining facial-recognition CCTV and a citizen reporting app, delivered under contract by a private technology vendor, Argus Systems. The platform has been running for eighteen months. 

You have been contracted by Meridian Estates Residents' Voice as an independent consultant, to help them understand the ethical, professional, and legal issues raised by SafeStreet ahead of their meeting with the Council, and to help them build a credible, well-evidenced case. Due to time constraints, you will only need to identify and address the most urgent issue, but you must support your analysis with relevant literature and an appropriate evaluation framework, such as the ones you have been practicing during this module. You will have to justify your choice and come up with an appropriate mitigation strategy.


## Letter

From: Meridian Estates Residents' Voice
To: Meridian City Council Community Engagement Team
Subject: Resident Concerns about SafeStreet

Dear Council Team,

We're writing on behalf of residents across Ashgrove, Thornfield and Percy House estates. Some of you know us from the community meetings we've been running since SafeStreet went in. We wanted to put our concerns in writing this time because we don't feel like they're being taken seriously, and we think people at the top of the Council need to hear this directly, not through a summary. We've also heard, secondhand, that some of the people who actually built this system had concerns of their own before it even launched, which makes us wonder who was listening to anyone at all.

When SafeStreet was first announced, we were told it would make our estates safer. Cameras everywhere, an app to report anything worrying, and some kind of smart system that helps police decide where to focus. Eighteen months in, here's what it actually feels like living with it.

People on Ashgrove and Percy House feel like they're being watched and flagged constantly, in a way that just doesn't happen on the other estates nearby with similar problems. We've compared notes with residents on Whitefield estate, which doesn't have anywhere near the camera coverage we do, and it's obvious something isn't fair here.

A few of us also did some digging and it turns out this "smart" system was trained on years of old police stop-and-search records. Most of us didn't need a computer to tell us those records were biased in the first place. We see the same patterns being repeated - many of our non-White residents have concerns that they’re getting flagged again and again.

Then there's the app. It lets anyone report a "risk" anonymously. We've heard from several Muslim families on Percy House that they get reported far more often around Eid and other religious occasions, for absolutely nothing - people gathering, visiting each other, cooking, normal life. It's being used to harass people, plain and simple, and nobody is checking whether reports are genuine before they go into whatever system decides who gets watched more.

We also want to know why our data is being kept for so long. We were told footage would be deleted after three months. Someone we know who used to do IT work looked into this informally and reckons footage is being kept for well over a year. We've also heard – and we really hope that this isn’t true - that some of it might have been shared with an insurance company. If our faces and our comings and goings are being sold on somehow, residents deserve to know.

If someone does get flagged and wants to challenge it, good luck. The only way to appeal is through an app, in English, and you need a smartphone and one of those Council digital ID logins. Mrs. Okafor on Thornfield is in her 80s, doesn't have a smartphone, and has been trying to get her son's flag removed for months with no way to even start the process. She's not the only one. This can't be the only route the Council offers.

And most worryingly, we've heard rumors (we'd like the Council to confirm or deny this properly) that there was some kind of security breach with the footage a few months back, involving contractors who work on reviewing it. If our footage was exposed to people who shouldn't have had access, residents have a right to be told. Finding out through gossip isn't good enough.

Lastly, and we know this is smaller compared to everything above, but several of us are also uncomfortable that nobody seems to have thought about what happens to all this equipment once it's outdated, or how much power it's using. It feels like nobody involved in setting this up thought past "does it work," to "should we be doing it this way."

We're not against feeling safe. Most of us want that. But right now SafeStreet feels like it was built for some residents and against others, and put up faster than anyone thought about whether it was fair, accessible, or secure.

We'd like a proper meeting with someone senior, not just the community liaison officer, to go through this.

Yours sincerely,
Meridian Estates Residents' Voice
On behalf of residents of Ashgrove, Thornfield and Percy House estates
